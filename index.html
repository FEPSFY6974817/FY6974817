Conference Paper: The Kessler Syndrome

Abstract

The Kessler syndrome, first described by Kessler and Cour-Palais (1979), describes a potential chain reaction between objects within the Earth’s orbit that generate debris, increasing the chances of further collisions in future which raise concerns for the safety of future space activities and missions. Here we discover the relationship between satellite congestion and the risks of the Kessler Syndrome whilst laying out the importance of orbital traffic coordination, satellite end-of-life disposal, and how aerospace engineers can integrate reliable spacecraft designs to mitigate space clutter, which can include redirecting debris effectively back to the Earth in safe ways. This article argues that growth in space activity must be accompanied by stronger engineering practices and international cooperation to preserve and sustain a clean and running orbital environment, as well as creating chance and potential to explore more in space.
